Pre-Mortem Analysis

What it is

A Pre-Mortem, developed by research psychologist Gary Klein, is the hypothetical dissection of a project before it has even launched. Unlike a standard "post-mortem" (which reviews a disaster after the damage is done), a pre-mortem begins by assuming the project has failed catastrophically in the future. By placing yourself in this imaginary future, you trigger hindsight bias in your brain, which makes identifying risks much easier and bypasses the social pressure to remain silent about potential issues during the optimistic kickoff phase.

Why it Works: Breaking Groupthink

During initial project kickoffs, team members are often reluctant to voice reservations because they don't want to sound unsupportive or negative. A pre-mortem flips this social dynamic: it challenges the team to be as creative as possible in finding ways the project could fail. In this framework, the person who surfaces the most realistic failure modes is seen as the most helpful contributor.

How to Fill out this Tool

1. Define the Goal: State the project name and launch deadline.

2. List Failure Modes: Brainstorm every possible scenario that could lead to total disaster (e.g., "the API credentials expired," "the key engineer left the company").

3. Design Prevention Actions: For every failure mode, write an actionable safeguard you can take today to prevent it. Any failure mode without a prevention plan will be highlighted in yellow as a critical risk item.

Real-world Examples

Example (Submitting a Critical Fellowship Application):
• Failure Mode: The recommendation letters from my professors were not uploaded in time, leading to immediate disqualification.
• Prevention Action (Today): Request the letters 6 weeks before the deadline, and set up a weekly check-in schedule to follow up.

• Failure Mode: The server crashed on the submission website during the final hour, preventing application submission.
• Prevention Action (Today): Set an internal hard deadline to submit the completed application 48 hours before the official cutoff.
