80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)

What it is

The Pareto Principle, formulated by Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto in 1896, states that roughly 80% of consequences come from 20% of the causes. In work and personal life, it means that 80% of your progress, output, or income is driven by just 20% of your activities. The goal of this tool is to calculate your Leverage Ratio (Results produced divided by Time spent) for each activity, identifying where you are highly leveraged and where you are caught in low-value busywork.

The Leverage Ratio Breakdown

For each activity, estimate your inputs and outputs. The tool will sort them descending by leverage ratio:

  • High Leverage (Your 20%): Activities where the results significantly exceed the time spent (Ratio > 1.5). These are your highest-impact moves. You should protect, double-down on, and prioritize these.
  • Low Leverage (Consider Cutting): Activities where the time spent significantly exceeds the results (Ratio < 0.7). This is busywork or administrative friction. You should seek to delegate, automate, or eliminate these.
  • Balanced (0.7 to 1.5): Standard, linear activities where effort matches returns. Maintain these at a baseline level.

Real-world Examples

Example A (Sales & Business Clients):
• Client A: Represents 10% of your weekly client calls, but generates 70% of your monthly recurring revenue (Leverage Ratio: 7.0) -> Your 20%. Protect this client at all costs.
• Client B: Represents 60% of your weekly customer support emails, but only represents 5% of monthly revenue (Leverage Ratio: 0.08) -> Consider Cutting.
Example B (Exam Preparation):
• Active Recall: Doing mock practice exams takes 20% of your study week but accounts for 85% of your final grades (Leverage Ratio: 4.25) -> Your 20%.
• Passive Reading: Highlighting the textbook takes 70% of your week but accounts for only 10% of final grade retention (Leverage Ratio: 0.14) -> Consider Cutting.
