Weighted Pros & Cons

What it is

The Pros & Cons list is the oldest decision tool in history, famously written about by Benjamin Franklin in a 1772 letter where he called it "Prudential Algebra." While basic lists are easy to compile, they treat all points as equal (e.g., "leaving friends" counts the same as "better coffee shops"). This tool adds Franklin's missing element: weighting. By scoring each argument from 1 to 3 based on its true significance, this tool compiles a weighted balance sheet and tilts a CSS scale, helping you visually separate major dealbreakers from minor conveniences.

Weighting Guidelines

Assign a weight to each entry based on its emotional, financial, or long-term significance:

  • Weight 1 (Minor): Nice-to-haves or minor inconveniences (e.g., "cooler weather," "must buy a parking pass").
  • Weight 2 (Significant): Strong factors that will noticeably affect your daily comfort or progress (e.g., "learning a new tech stack," "30-minute longer commute").
  • Weight 3 (Critical): Core dealbreakers or massive life-changers (e.g., "doubling my salary," "moving far away from close family").

The Gut Check Nudge

Numbers are only half the story. Pay close attention to your immediate emotional reaction to the tilted scale. If the scale tilts toward "Cons" but you feel a wave of disappointment, your subconscious actually wants to choose "Yes." Don't ignore what the gut check reveals.

Real-world Examples

Example (Relocating for a Job Offer):
• Pros: 40% salary raise (Weight: 3) | Exciting tech project (Weight: 2) | Sunny coastal weather (Weight: 1).
• Total Pros Weight: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6.
• Cons: Leaving my tight-knit local friend group (Weight: 3) | High cost of housing in the new city (Weight: 3).
• Total Cons Weight: 3 + 3 = 6.
• Verdict: The scale is perfectly balanced at 6 vs 6, revealing the true gravity of the trade-off. It's not a clear "yes" just because of the salary.
