5 Whys Root Cause Analysis

What it is

Developed by Sakichi Toyoda, the founder of Toyota, the 5 Whys is an iterative interrogative technique used to explore the cause-and-effect relationships underlying a particular problem. The primary goal of the technique is to determine the root cause of a defect or problem by repeating the question "Why?" five times. Each answer forms the basis of the next question, peeling back layers of symptoms to reveal the systemic issue below.

How to run a 5 Whys Audit

1. Define the Symptom: Start by writing down the specific problem exactly as it happened. Keep it fact-based.

2. Trace the Chain: Ask "Why did this happen?" and write down the immediate direct cause. Repeat this process. Do not skip steps; make sure the logical connection from one node to the next is direct and undeniable.

3. Systemic over Human: Avoid stopping at "human error" (e.g., "John forgot to check"). John forgot to check because there was no checklist, or the workflow allowed single-point failures. Look for system and process failures.

4. Determine the Stop Point: Stop when you hit a process failure that you have the power to change or fix. That is your root cause.

Real-world Examples

Example A (System Outage):
• Symptom: The database crashed and took down the site.
• Why 1: The disk drive filled up completely.
• Why 2: Large temp files were not being deleted.
• Why 3: The cron script that clears temp files failed.
• Why 4: The script relied on a legacy database driver that was removed.
• Why 5 (Root Cause): We do not have a dependency management policy for system integrations.
Example B (Personal Habit):
• Symptom: I missed my dentist appointment.
• Why 1: I woke up an hour late.
• Why 2: My phone battery died overnight, so my alarm didn't ring.
• Why 3: I forgot to plug my phone in before sleeping.
• Why 4: My charger is in the living room and I fell asleep reading in bed.
• Why 5 (Root Cause): I do not have a consistent bedtime routine or a secondary alarm system.
