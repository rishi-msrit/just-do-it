SMART Goals

What it is

Introduced by George T. Doran in 1981, SMART Goals are a structural framework designed to take vague, fuzzy aspirations and turn them into concrete, trackable commitments. By forcing yourself to articulate exactly what, how, when, and why you will do something, you dramatically increase the likelihood of execution.

The SMART Breakdown

To write a solid goal, fill out each letter deliberately:

  • Specific: Focus on a narrow, clear action. Avoid vague words like "improve," "get better," or "more." Instead of "Learn code," write "Learn Rust syntax and build a command-line tool."
  • Measurable: Choose a metric that proves completion. How will you know you succeeded? (e.g., "spending 1 hour daily," "reading 3 chapters," "writing 5 CLI tools").
  • Achievable: Be honest about your limits. Given your current schedule, skills, and energy levels, is this realistic? How does it bridge from what you already know?
  • Relevant: Align the goal with your larger life or career objectives. Why does this goal matter to you today?
  • Time-bound: Set a concrete deadline or regular cadence. Without a target date (e.g., "by October 15th"), tasks stretch out indefinitely.

Real-world Examples

Example A (Software Engineering):
• Vague: I want to get better at systems programming.
• SMART: I will learn Rust fundamentals and build a CLI tool (Specific) by October 15th (Time-bound), measured by coding 1 hour daily and completing 5 CLI test projects (Measurable) because it helps prepare for my systems engineering module next term (Relevant). This is achievable because I already know Python and JavaScript (Achievable).
Example B (Personal Fitness):
• Vague: I want to get fit.
• SMART: I will run a 5k distance (Specific) by December 1st (Time-bound), measured by following a Couch-to-5K app program 3 days a week (Measurable), because I want to improve my cardiovascular health and run a local charity race (Relevant). This is achievable because I am starting at a 2k baseline and have 12 weeks to build endurance (Achievable).
