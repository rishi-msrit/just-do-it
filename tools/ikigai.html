Ikigai (生き甲斐)

What it is

Ikigai is a Japanese concept meaning "a reason for being"—essentially, the thing that makes you want to jump out of bed in the morning. Originating from Okinawa (a region famous for longevity), this framework helps you locate your career sweet spot. It is modeled as a Venn diagram of four overlapping circles. While listing your thoughts in each circle is the first step, the true power of the model lies in mapping the intersections.

The Intersection Zones

  • Love ∩ Good At = Passion: Brings satisfaction and excitement, but you might struggle to make a living unless you find a market.
  • Good At ∩ Paid For = Profession: Keeps you comfortable and financially secure, but can feel dry, clinical, or empty if you don't care about the work.
  • Paid For ∩ World Needs = Vocation: A useful job that pays the bills, but you might feel a constant, nagging desire for personal growth or self-expression.
  • World Needs ∩ Love = Mission: Brings immense fulfillment and purpose, but without pay, you risk burning out or struggling financially.
  • The Center = Ikigai: A rare alignment of all four circles. Achieving this means you are doing work you love, that you excel at, that sustains you financially, and that contributes positively to the world.

Real-world Examples

Example A (Developer Advocate):
• Love: Teaching others, coding minor experiments, traveling.
• Good at: Technical writing, public speaking, API prototyping.
• World Needs: Clean, understandable developer documentation and open-source tooling libraries.
• Paid for: A Developer Relations Advocate role at a cloud hosting provider.
• Ikigai Alignment: Building tutorials that developers love and getting paid by the host.
Example B (Sustainable Farmer):
• Love: Working outdoors, organic gardening, quiet environments.
• Good at: Soil biology, crop rotation planning, composting techniques.
• World Needs: Pesticide-free local produce, restoring agricultural soil health.
• Paid for: Selling organic vegetable crates to local weekly subscribers.
• Ikigai Alignment: Running a micro-farm supplying the community.
