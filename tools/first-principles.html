      First Principles Thinking

      What it is

      First Principles Thinking—associated with Aristotle and popularized by Elon Musk—is the act of boiling a challenge down to its most basic, foundational truths that you know are factually and physically true, and then building a custom solution up from scratch. This contrasts with "reasoning by analogy," which is copying what others do with minor tweaks, inherited beliefs, or rules of thumb.

      How to think in First Principles

      1. Identify and Define the Goal: Clearly write down what you are trying to build or solve.

      2. List Your Current Assumptions: Write down the beliefs or rules of thumb you hold about the problem. Check the ones that are merely standard conventions rather than absolute physical laws (e.g., "batteries are expensive," "we must rent an office").

      3. Deconstruct to Bedrock Truths: Identify the foundational components. What are the raw materials, human behaviors, or basic costs that are physically or mathematically inarguable? (e.g., raw metals exchange prices, basic web hosting fees).

      4. Rebuild from the Bedrock: Using only the truths you listed in step 3, design a new path that bypasses the challenged assumptions.

      Real-world Examples

      Example A (SpaceX Rocket Costs):
      • Assumption: Buying a rocket is too expensive ($65M). I can't start a space company.
      • Bedrock Truths: Rockets are made of carbon fiber, titanium, aerospace-grade aluminum, and fuel. The raw materials cost only 2% of the retail rocket price.
      • Rebuilt Solution: SpaceX decided to buy raw materials directly and build the rockets themselves, reducing manufacturing costs by 90% and implementing reusability.
      Example B (Creating a Coffee Brand):
      • Assumption: Starting a coffee brand requires renting a storefront, buying a $15k espresso machine, and hiring baristas ($100k startup cost).
      • Bedrock Truths: People buy coffee beans to drink at home. You can roast beans in small batches, build a simple Shopify store, and ship direct-to-consumer.
      • Rebuilt Solution: Start an online-only subscription coffee brand from a residential kitchen with zero storefront overhead.
